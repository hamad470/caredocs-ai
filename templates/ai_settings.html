One model, several keys
This system talks to Gemini and nothing else. An earlier version could also call Claude or Groq and fell through from one to the next when a call failed, which was convenient and wrong: a care note would record that a model wrote the text without recording which model, and an evaluation run could be served by two different model families because one free tier hit its daily quota partway through.

Several keys are still supported, and that is a different thing. Every key reaches the same models, so rotating between them changes whether the system can answer, never what it answers.

How AI generation works here
A model is used in exactly three places: drafting the free-text narrative on the care note, incident, handover, wellbeing and care plan forms; answering questions in Ask the Records; and nothing else. The fall-risk model is a scikit-learn classifier trained locally and never calls an API. The medication administration record is a verbatim audit trail with no model in its path.

Keys are stored in ai_config.json in the app folder and applied immediately — no restart required. They are sent to Google and to nobody else.

Without a key the app still works. Every narrative field falls back to a deterministic template, which is plainer prose and a complete record.

Governance: every AI-generated record is flagged ai_generated = True and enters as pending approval. A named human must approve it before it becomes part of the clinical record. The model drafts; the registered professional remains the author.

This system uses synthetic data only. Do not enter real resident information. Free API tiers may retain and train on submitted data, which would be an unlawful processing of health data under UK GDPR Article 9 — and the multi-key rotation above exists precisely to stay inside those free tiers.
