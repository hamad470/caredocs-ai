How it works

A question passes through five stages. Everything up to the language model runs in your browser; only the question and the selected evidence lines are sent to Gemini.

  1. Understand

    Rules extract the resident, the time period, the record type and the kind of question: search, table, count or trend.

  2. Filter

    Records outside those filters are removed before any search runs, so another resident's notes can never be retrieved.

  3. Retrieve

    BM25 ranks the remaining records; maximal marginal relevance drops near-duplicates. Counts, trends and tables are computed over every matching record instead.

  4. Generate

    Gemini receives only the numbered evidence lines and must cite an evidence ID for every sentence.

  5. Verify

    Each sentence's content words and numbers are looked up in the lines it cites. Numbers must match exactly.

Evaluation

Measured offline on the full pipeline in the accompanying MSc dissertation (Chapter 7), over the same 25,017 passages. The in-browser demo uses the BM25 stage, and its rankings are tested to match the Python implementation exactly.

Limitations

  • All records are synthetic. Nothing here is evidence about real care homes.
  • The claim checker detects invented content but cannot tell whose record a sentence came from (52% of another resident's notes pass). That is why the resident filter runs before retrieval rather than relying on checks afterwards.
  • Question understanding is rule-based. Unusual phrasings fall back to plain search.
  • Retrieval quality is a known-item benchmark, not clinician-judged relevance.
